## Findings
Composition: big simple masses in layers (foreground near-black, mid hills, pale far hills), the subject tiny against large calm space; the Goose look repeats one flat colour per object with shape doing all the work, so a flock reads as many near-identical rounded blobs with a few outliers. Camera: Alto is a side-on locked frame with parallax depth; Goose is a fixed high three-quarter angle that pulls out wide and is criticised when it fights the player (reviews), so keep a steady high angle, soft follow, zoom-out available. Lighting and colour: a single low sun with a hazy glow, haze lifting distant layers toward the sky colour, dark teal/indigo shadows rather than black, one warm accent (sun, fire) against cool dusk; the wolf as the darkest shape in the frame against the lightest sky. Production design: flat unlit fills, tiny palette per scene, geometric trees (triangle pines, teardrops), no outlines in these two games, so the ink outline, paper grain and woodcut typography are the added storybook layer (Eichenberg-style wood engraving: hard black/white, hatched tone). Motion: Alto is calm gliding with drifting snow diamonds; Goose animation is expressive and comic, with small tick marks for noise; for the game use squash-and-stretch on sheep, stillness as tension, and a held frame at the pounce. Sound: Goose trailer is scored with Debussy preludes (stop-start piano), Alto is soft ambient piano and pads; use sparse piano or pad at dusk, a low sting at the pounce, wool-soft thumps, silence before the reveal. Motifs: sun/moon disc, bunting, campfire, repeated trees, tiny lone figure, flock rhythm. Materials: matte paper and gouache, flat fills with scalloped soft edges, grain overlay, hard ink line, stamped slightly misregistered type; no gloss, no gradients except sky and haze.
